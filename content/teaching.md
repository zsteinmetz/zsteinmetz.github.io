My teaching is characterized by interactivity and intensive individual support. The close integration of my teaching and research makes theoretical content tangible for students, motivates further exploration, and enables them to participate in current research topics at the institute. Since 2015, I have taught about 30 courses (roughly 100 SWS, i.e., semester hours) in English and German, and have supervised numerous theses (B.Sc. and M.Sc.). Several of my former students are now pursuing or have completed a PhD.

For instance, I completely redesigned my lecture *Advanced Environmental Chemistry* as a flipped classroom — short videos, exercises with sample solutions, and a case study for self-directed preparation, concluded by joint in-person sessions with quiz questions and journal-club-style paper discussions.

## Courses

My teaching covers environmental chemistry, environmental analysis, and quantitative methods.

### Environmental chemistry and analysis

- **Advanced Environmental Chemistry** (M.Sc.) — lecture and tutorial on partitioning, transport, and transformation processes of organic and inorganic pollutant classes in soil, water, and air, including QSAR approaches and the discussion of current research articles.
- **Environmental Analysis** (B.Sc., taught in German) — laboratory course on sampling soils and waters, basic parameters (pH, conductivity, redox potential, turbidity), BOD/COD, soil water content and texture, PAH analysis by HPLC with UV/fluorescence detection, BTEX by GC/MS, copper and zinc by AAS, and microplastic identification by ATR-FTIR spectroscopy; the lecture and accompanying seminars cover theoretical foundations, computer-assisted data entry, evaluation, and statistics.

### Methods, statistics, and data

- **Introduction to Methods in Natural and Environmental Sciences** (B.Sc., taught in German) — exercise and laboratory course introducing scientific working methods, including computer-assisted data evaluation and statistics; I contributed to developing its thematic and didactic concept.
- **Interdisciplinary Introduction to Scientific Methods** (M.Sc.) — module on scientific research methods, statistics, and data analysis; I developed the thematic and didactic concept and contributed to the accreditation process.
- **R for Beginners** — tutorial introducing R for statistical data analysis.

## Teaching methods

- **Flipped classroom:** preparation and independent work before the joint session; short videos, exercises, case studies, and concluding in-person sessions.
- **Journal club:** contextualizing current research and critical discussion of scientific papers.
- **Laboratory exercises:** connecting theory, measurement, and data interpretation.
- **Data analysis exercises:** working with R, OpenSpecy, or OpenChrom.
- **Research-based assignments:** working on real environmental science questions.
- **Tutorials:** targeted support with foundational concepts; senior students can actively help shape teaching under my guidance.

## Supervision

I supervise undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral projects in environmental chemistry, analytical chemistry, soil science, environmental pollutants, and data analysis. Two doctoral projects are currently underway — on methods for identifying and quantifying hydrophilic polymers in soil and on microplastic background levels in agricultural soils. In addition, nine Master's theses and two Bachelor's theses were completed under my supervision; I regularly act as second assessor and external advisor.

Possible thesis topics include microplastics in soils, polymer characterization, analytical method development, PFAS analysis, environmental data analysis, and reproducible workflows.

In supervising project and degree theses, a research plan plays a central role: it helps students develop and sharpen their own ideas in dialogue, provides an intensive introduction to the required analytical instruments and safe laboratory work, and makes the requirements for the thesis transparent. Alongside subject knowledge, students acquire practical skills in project planning, scientific working, and computer-based statistical methods.

## Open science in teaching

I introduce students to open-source software and reproducible workflows where appropriate. This helps students understand not only scientific results, but also how those results are generated, documented, and communicated. It also creates valuable links to professional practice — from data processing with R or OpenChrom to geospatial analysis with QGIS.
